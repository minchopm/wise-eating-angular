Baby Feeding

Why calcium-rich meals matter for your baby every day

Calcium supports building and maintaining strong bones and teeth. It also helps with blood vessel constriction and relaxation, muscle contraction, normal nervous system function, and hormone secretion—and it plays a role in connective tissue formation.

Quick note: This content is for general education. Always check with your pediatrician when you’re introducing new foods.

What is the Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) for calcium for your baby?

Based on the references listed below, the Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) for calcium is 210 mg for babies from 0 to 0.5 months, 270 mg for babies from 0.5 to 1 year old, and 500 mg for toddlers between 1 to 3 years old.

It’s important to keep in mind that for good calcium absorption, you should also feed your baby foods rich in vitamins D and K. Magnesium matters, too—and it should be about half of the quantity you feed him/her of calcium.

Which foods are rich in calcium?

Some of the richest foods in calcium are dairy products like yoghurt and milk, low-fat Ricotta cheese, Swiss cheese, Cheddar cheese, white cheese, yellow cheese, and mozzarella.

Other good sources of calcium include white beans, chickpeas, and salmon. Vegetables like steamed spinach are also rich in calcium, along with steamed broccoli, egg yolk, potatoes, and sweet potatoes.
